CHANGELOG OF THE SUPER MARIO RPG PORT (smrpg-recomp)
====================================================
Newest first. Every published change is a new version, with its own tag and release on GitHub (in the public repository,
since 0.51.0-beta: earlier ones are only described here).

Numbering (since 0.45.1-beta): x.y.z-alpha / x.y.z-beta, Semantic Versioning 2.0.0 (semver.org):
  - z, patch: fixes only, nothing new and nothing that changes how it is used (0.45.0 -> 0.45.1);
  - y, minor: something new, without breaking what was there (0.45.1 -> 0.46.0; the patch goes back to 0);
  - x, major: something that breaks what came before (saves, settings or files that stop working, something removed);
    with 0.x (development) that bumps y too. 1.0.0 will be the first finished, stable version, with no suffix.
  - the suffix, maturity: alpha while it was not playable (up to 0.20.0), beta since it is fully playable (0.21.0).
Earlier versions were called v0 to v45: vN is 0.N.0 (alpha up to 20, beta from 21 on).

0.50.3-beta (2026-11-20) — battle step pacing, fixed: 0 steps on frames with no step due
  1. The bug (found by the review of 0.50.2, which was not published): rt_battle_plan returned 0 in TWO different
     cases — "pacing does not apply" and "it applies but no step is due on this frame" — and the caller treated both as
     "business as usual" and took ONE update_common step: at 120/240 the logic still ran at the speed of the limit (240
     steps per second at 240) plus the planned ones; the counter only counted the planned ones and the measurement did
     not see it. The plan now returns -1 (does not apply: the original path) or 0..3 (0: this frame does NOT call
     update_common).
  2. Measured with an independent counter of the ACTUAL calls to update_common (counted by whoever makes them, on the
     original path too; it shows in the log when the battle ends, next to the planned steps: they have to match):
       limit 60:  24.09 s of battle, 1395 steps = 1395 calls, 57.9 per second
       limit 60:  20.89 s, 1209 = 1209, 57.9
       limit 120: 20.38 s, 1164 = 1164, 57.1
       limit 240: 25.14 s, 1432 = 1432, 57.0
       limit 240: 20.78 s, 1182 = 1182, 56.9
     Drawing runs at the limit during the battle (60.0 / 120.0 / 240.0 fps). The control run with LARECOMP_BATTLE_FIX=0
     at 60 (the original: one step per frame, no pacing) gives 21.31 s and 1176 calls, 55.2 per second: the battle
     itself already skipped 8% of its Update phases (its internal loads), so "~60 per second" is really "whatever the
     game gives", and pacing is on par with or above the original at every limit.
  3. Duration with the same script: 20.4-25.1 s in ALL runs, limits mixed — the difference between limits (60: 24.09;
     120: 20.38; 240: 25.14) is WITHIN the spread between two identical runs (60: 24.09 vs 20.89; 240: 25.14 vs 20.78).
     What moves the duration is the phase of the button presses relative to the step timeline: the battle entry
     transition stalls one frame for ~900 ms (946/874/831/835 depending on the run, an engine thing) and that stall
     trims a different amount of debt each time, so the first timed hit lands at a different point of its window and the
     battle is settled with a different number of landed hits (1209-1432 steps). With the original (one step per frame,
     no pacing) the same route gives 21.31 s: within the same range.
  4. No press is lost, not even on frames with no step: at 240 the button mashing is 8 ms presses (less than half a
     step) and the battles end in the same time range with the same number of steps — a lost press would leave a menu
     waiting for the next second and drag it out. The opening of the battle (up to the first phase divergence) matches
     across limits: captures at the same wall-clock time differ by 2-4.3% (the noise of the fast animations) and the
     bright-text peak of the first hit window shows up in all three: 2184/2158/2186 pixels at t=3330 (units of 60).
     LARECOMP_BATTLE_FPS=30 and LARECOMP_BATTLE_FIX=0 (which now also logs its battle and its call rate) work as before;
     battle_slow (LSFG) is still consistent. Test: a battle script at several limits and its analysis, outside the
     repository (it now counts the actual calls and their rate).

0.50.2-beta (2026-11-20) — battles draw at the "FPS limit", with their logic decoupled from drawing
  1. Until now, for as long as a battle lasted the vsync (hle/vi.c) was forced to 60 Hz: its logic advances one 1/60 s
     step per frame, and with more frames the battle would run faster than its animations and its sound. The vsync now
     ticks at the "FPS limit" (120, 144, 240...) in battle too, and the BattleManager.update_common steps are paced by
     BattleManager.update (main 0x7001cd9250, overridden in tools/game_overrides.c) by real time, with the accumulator
     in hle/battle_rate.c: each frame takes 0, 1, 2 or 3 steps depending on the accumulated time (the debt is capped at
     4 steps so that it does not spiral if a frame takes very long), and the logic still takes exactly 60 steps per
     second, like the console. The menu text ("FPS limit") and the comment in battle_rate.c say so.
  2. Presses are not lost between steps: the controller snapshot (ControllerObserver._npadState: buttons and preButtons)
     is looked at once per frame, and whatever was pressed or released since the previous step is held and delivered to
     the next step due, as if the button had been down during that whole console frame (GetButtonDown = buttons &
     ~preButtons, computed on the fly: the step sees the edges injected into the snapshot, which is restored
     afterwards).
  3. (Superseded by 0.50.3: this build had a bug that the review found — the plan's caller took ONE step on frames with
     no planned step, so at 120/240 the logic still ran at the speed of the limit and that build's measurements were not
     valid.)
  4. Whatever is time-based stays the same: the BattleCharacterAnimator animators run on real time (only a slow frame
     slows them down, as before) and captures at the same wall-clock time come out in the same pose at every limit; the
     sound comes from the steps (60 per second, like the console). The logic's movement (the positions of the battle
     characters) advances at 60 steps per second: at 240 the positions change every 4 frames, with the console's
     granularity; the animators interpolate in between and no stutter shows in the captures.
  5. LARECOMP_BATTLE_FPS=30 (debug mode: vsync at 60 Hz and two steps per frame, as before: "[battle] ... debug mode",
     the vsync drops to 60 and comes back on exit) and LARECOMP_BATTLE_FIX=0 (one step per frame at the limit: the old
     sped-up battle, for comparison: no [battle] lines, at 240 the logic ran at ~210-240 steps per second) still work.
     Frame generation (vk_lsfg.c battle_slow) is still consistent: the battle needs 60 real frames in which to take its
     60 steps, and if they do not fit it is turned off while the battle lasts, as before. Tests: a separate battle
     script (the same route in real time at any limit) and its analysis (duration, steps per second, capture matching
     and text peaks). Recorded in STATUS.md.

0.50.0-beta (2026-11-20) — Lossless Scaling frame generation inside the game (LSFG 3.1)

     (generation stays off and the menu says so). The swapchain runs in FIFO with N+1 extra images; the generated frames

  2. Measured on an RX 9070 XT (240 Hz display), d01f1b at 1280x720, x2 generation, flow 75%, quality: GPU 1.07-1.22 ms

     without Lossless.dll: the game runs the same (60.0 fps, 0 skipped, "[lsfg] off"), and the capture of the same frame
     with generation on and off does not change (mean 1.4 / 1.26% of pixels, the noise band of that area's animation).

  2. Everything it needs ships with it, in builder\ (316 MB): builder.dll; LLVM, clang and lld (the MSYS2 DLLs and the
     ones they load); the embedded Python with the generator; the clang headers (all of them) and, of the MinGW-w64
     ones, the 22 that the game code includes (obtained with clang -MD for x86-64-v3, -v2 and x86-64, and verified by

     SSE4. Not tested on a real CPU without AVX2 (nor the AMD FSR DLLs, which barely use AVX and check the CPU with
     CPUID).
  4. What came up while testing it (with gdb):
     - the stack: clang and LLVM recurse very deep in the huge functions of the generated code
       (MemorySSAUpdater::recursePhi); with the 2 MB of a normal thread, the process crashed without warning about
       halfway through (0xC00000FD). MSYS2's clang.exe runs with 16 MB and zig with 46: the builder's threads reserve
       64 MB (address space only) and run at below-normal priority, so that the PC stays responsive;
     - LLVM's CrashRecoveryContext (on MinGW, an exception handler for the whole process) was disabled entirely by the
       access faults that unicorn triggers and catches on purpose. There is now a safety net of our own, only on the
       compilation threads: a fault inside clang, or a fatal LLVM error (which would call exit and close the installer),
       becomes an error of that file. LLVM's is turned on only during linking, where lld needs it (on a fatal error, lld
       calls exit);
     - the process hung on exit: lld's thread pool (llvm::parallel) stayed alive and its static destructor waited for
       threads that Windows had already killed. lld links with --threads=1 (0.6 s) and, just in case, the installer
       terminates outright if it got to load the builder (its logs are unbuffered).
  5. Canceling returns right away: the threads finish the file they had in hand on their own (the largest takes ~3 min)
     and installing again waits for them. Closing the window closes the entire process.

  presented: on screen, twice the frames per second. It also needs amd_fidelityfx_framegeneration_vk.dll next to the

  place or swaps it for the normal one.

  Way) and planes (0.3-0.6 to 1000). FSR now uses those planes and that field of view too.

  two frames when the camera moved vertically (and FG 3.1 fell back on its optical flow); measured in captures of what
  is presented (LARECOMP_FG_PSHOT): with Y inverted, the generated frame lands halfway (2.2 of 4.5 px vertically, 5.2 of
  10.5 horizontally). FSR, with Y inverted, gets worse (more error against the native render): it only changes for frame
  generation.

  generated frames: the window is copied before the runtime menu is drawn and AMD uses the copy so as not to interpolate
  the counter (HUDLessColor). Not with HDR (AMD dropped it from the generated frames): it is interpolated with the
  scene.

  the driver crashed at startup): the count is now queried first. HDR prefers A2B10G10R10 (frame generation does not

  The 0.29.0-beta sweep (tools/shader_sweep.c, 82,032 shaders) left 156 untranslated; 130 were bindless textures whose
  handle the tracing could not follow (runtime/nvn/maxwell/translate.c):
  1. HSETP2, DSETP, VSETP, KIL, BPT, CCTL, RED, SUST, ST, R2B and SETCRSPTR do not write a register in their bits 0-7

     that is how the NVN compiler guards the textures it only reads if the material uses them.
  3. A write with the predicate opposite to the use's (@!P0 MOV r24 ... @P0 TEX.B r24) is not its definition.
  4. A handle saved in local memory and reloaded (STL / LDL) is followed to what was saved.
